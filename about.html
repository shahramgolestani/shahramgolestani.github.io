Biography

Dr. Shahram Golestani is an economist, researcher, and writer whose work focuses on political economy, monetary economics, energy markets, and international strategic relations. He holds a Ph.D. in Economics from the University of Tehran, with doctoral specialization in monetary economics and econometrics. His doctoral research examined the strategic interaction between OPEC and OECD countries through the framework of a bilateral monopoly game, combining economic theory, game theory, and energy policy analysis.

Over the course of his academic career, Dr. Golestani has taught economics, econometrics, business statistics, and quantitative methods at both university and college levels. He served as a faculty member and Assistant Professor of Economics at Shahid Bahonar University of Kerman for a decade and has continued teaching in Canada since 2018. His teaching experience spans undergraduate, graduate, and professional education, with a particular interest in helping students understand the connection between economic theory and real-world economic challenges.

His research covers a broad range of topics including monetary systems, energy economics, natural gas and oil markets, game-theoretic analysis of international economic relations, financial markets, governance, and public policy. He has published scholarly work on OPEC, the Gas Exporting Countries Forum (GECF), oil- price dynamics, financial markets, exchange rates, corruption, healthcare policy, and economic development. His research frequently combines quantitative methods with institutional and political analysis to better understand the interaction between economic incentives, power structures, and strategic decision-making.

In addition to academic research, Dr. Golestani writes on issues related to global political economy, energy geopolitics, international power relations, and the evolving structure of the world economy. His current interests focus on the relationship between economic systems and political power, the strategic role of energy resources in international affairs, and the application of game theory to economic and geopolitical conflicts.

Dr. Golestani currently serves as a member of the Editorial Board of the Socio-Economic Journal of the Canadian Institute for Middle East and Gulf Studies and continues to pursue independent research and writing in economics and political economy.

Current Research Focus

My current research focuses on the application of game theory to international economic and geopolitical interactions. I am particularly interested in strategic behavior among states, energy producers, and major economic powers in an increasingly multipolar global system.

My research examines the dynamics of the global economy, the structure and evolution of international energy markets, and the relationship between economic interests and political power. A major area of interest is the global oil market, including the strategic behavior of producers, consumers, and state actors in response to economic, political, and security challenges.

I also study developments in the Middle East and their implications for global energy security, international political economy, and the balance of power among regional and global actors. Through the use of game-theoretic models and political-economic analysis, I seek to better understand the strategic foundations of cooperation, competition, conflict, and economic transformation in the contemporary world.

Research Philosophy

My research is guided by the belief that economic outcomes cannot be fully understood without considering the strategic behavior of individuals, organizations, and states. Economics, political behavior, and institutional structures are deeply interconnected, and meaningful analysis requires an examination of both economic incentives and the strategic interactions that shape decision-making.

Game theory provides an important framework for understanding cooperation, competition, conflict, and negotiation in economic and political systems. I am particularly interested in applying game-theoretic reasoning to international political economy, global energy markets, and geopolitical developments, where the actions of one actor often depend on the expectations and responses of others.

At the same time, I believe that theoretical analysis should be complemented by empirical evidence. Quantitative methods, econometrics, and data analysis play a central role in evaluating economic relationships, testing hypotheses, and understanding complex real-world phenomena. My approach seeks to combine theoretical rigor with empirical investigation in order to better explain economic and political developments at both national and international levels.

Ultimately, my goal is to contribute to a deeper understanding of how strategic behavior, economic forces, and political institutions interact to shape the contemporary global economy.
