Research Overview

My research examines the interaction between economic incentives, political behavior, and strategic decision-making. Drawing on game theory, political economy, and quantitative methods, I study how states, institutions, firms, and other actors respond to changing economic and geopolitical environments.

A central theme of my work is the strategic nature of economic and political interactions. My research explores how cooperation, competition, negotiation, and conflict shape outcomes in global markets, international relations, and energy systems.

Research Areas

Game Theory

Application of game-theoretic models to economic behavior, international relations, strategic competition, and geopolitical interactions.

Political Economy

Analysis of the relationship between economic systems, political institutions, power structures, and public policy.

Global Economy

Study of international economic relations, globalization, trade, financial systems, and the evolving structure of the world economy.

Energy Economics and Geopolitics

Research on global oil and natural gas markets, OPEC, energy security, resource competition, and the strategic role of energy in international affairs.

Monetary Economics

Investigation of monetary systems, financial structures, inflation dynamics, exchange rates, and central banking.

Econometrics and Quantitative Analysis

Application of statistical and econometric methods to the study of economic, financial, and political phenomena.

Current Research Themes

  • Game-theoretic analysis of geopolitical competition
  • Global oil market dynamics and strategic behavior
  • Political economy of the Middle East
  • International energy security
  • Economic power and geopolitical influence
  • Quantitative analysis of economic and political systems
